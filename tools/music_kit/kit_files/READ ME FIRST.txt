RED ROGUE MUSIC TESTER
======================

Make a MIDI in your music program (MuseScore, FL Studio, anything that exports
.mid), drag it onto "DROP YOUR SONG HERE", and the game opens playing your song
on the title screen AND in every battle. No install needed.

UPDATING FROM AN OLDER KIT
-------------------------
This is kit version 5. Extract it into a NEW folder and use its
"DROP YOUR SONG HERE.bat". Keep your songs; do not copy the old _engine over
the new one. Updating only the DLS/SF2 does not update the tester.
New in version 5:
  - Your song plays in battles too. See HEARING IT IN BATTLE below.
  - The game opens in BGB (a more accurate Game Boy emulator) instead of mGBA.
    Its keys are different; see HEARING IT IN BATTLE.
  - "Game Songs" folder: every song in the game, with a sheet listing each
    channel's instruments. See GAME SONGS below.
  - The window warns when Ch1 looks like your melody. See WHICH TRACK GETS
    THE MELODY below: it explains notes dropping out in battle.
From version 4: Ch3 follows three velocity bands: 1-42 = 25%, 43-84 = 50%,
85-127 = 100%. For an older song to keep its full-volume Ch3, use 85-127.


QUICK START
-----------
1. Before extracting the zip: right-click it, Properties, tick "Unblock", OK.
   If Windows says it protected your PC, click "More info", then "Run anyway".
2. Open Template.mid in your program. It is a small working song with the four
   tracks already named, plus a Loop track that says where the song repeats
   from (see LOOPING below). Write your song in them.
3. Export as a MIDI file (Type 1 / multi-track, not Type 0, if it asks).
4. Drag the .mid onto "DROP YOUR SONG HERE.bat". A black window shows what
   happens, then the game opens. The song starts after the intro. Press Start
   (Enter) to skip the intro. To hear it in a battle, see HEARING IT IN BATTLE.
5. When a song is finished, send us the zip that appears in the folder
   "SEND THESE TO US" (open ATTRIBUTION.txt inside it first and fill in your
   name and the song title). It holds your original file, what the tester made
   from it, and the kit version, so we can put it in the game exactly as you
   heard it. Nothing from _engine. "KIT VERSION.txt" says which kit you have.
   "INSTRUMENTS AND DRUMS.txt" lists every sound at a glance.


THE FOUR TRACKS (it is a Game Boy, so there are only four voices)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Ch1  Square wave. Harmony or second line (sound effects borrow it; see below).
Ch2  Square wave. Melody or lead.
Ch3  Soft wave. Usually bass.
Ch4  Noise. Drums only.

Every channel sounds at the pitch written in your program.

WHICH TRACK GETS THE MELODY
  The game's sound effects borrow the music's voices while they play. The
  beep every time the player presses A and most menu sounds take over Ch1.
  Attack sounds in battle take Ch4 most often, then Ch1, then Ch2. Pokemon
  cries take Ch1, Ch2 and Ch4 for a moment. While a sound effect plays, that
  channel's notes are silent, and a long note that was cut does not come
  back: the channel stays quiet until its next note starts.
  Ch1 is borrowed far more often than Ch2, which is why Pokemon games put
  the melody on Ch2 and the harmony on Ch1 (Gold/Silver/Crystal's battle
  themes sit 3-8 notes higher on Ch2). A melody on Ch1 loses notes every
  time the player presses A, which is very noticeable in battle. Busy drum
  parts lose hits during attacks for the same reason.
  The tester warns you: "Note: Ch1 sits higher than Ch2 ... so Ch1 is
  probably your melody." It is a hint (it compares the average pitch), not
  an error.
  To move the melody: swap the names of your Ch1 and Ch2 tracks (for
  example "Ch1 Square 25" becomes "Ch2 Square 25" and the old Ch2 becomes
  Ch1), export, and drop the song again. The sounds and volumes travel with
  the track names, so nothing else changes. In a .asm song, swap the two
  label names on the "channel 1," and "channel 2," lines at the top instead,
  and move the "tempo" line (and "volume", if any) to the top of the part
  that is now channel 1: channel 1 starts first, so it must set the tempo.
  The tester stops with a message if it doesn't.
  Then listen in a battle (next section) and press A a lot: the melody
  should keep going and only the harmony should blink out.

HOW THE TESTER FINDS YOUR TRACKS
  - Any track with Ch1, Ch2, Ch3 or Ch4 in its name is used for that voice.
    "Ch1 Square 50" and "Ch3 Wave 2 bass" both work. A track named Loop sets
    the loop point. Other tracks are ignored (the window tells you which).
  - If no track is named like that, it uses your tracks in order: the first
    three instruments are Ch1, Ch2, Ch3, and the drums (the track on MIDI
    channel 10, where MuseScore puts its drumset) are Ch4. If your drums are
    on another channel, name the track Ch4.
  - More than three non-drum tracks and no names: it stops and asks you to
    merge some or name the ones you want.

PICKING THE SOUND: put it in the track name
  Ch1 / Ch2:  "Square 12.5", "Square 25", "Square 50" or "Square 75"
              (how thin the pulse is). Or "Duty 0" to "Duty 3" (same four,
              in that order). With neither, you get 12.5%.
  Ch3:        "Wave 0" to "Wave 14" (15 different shapes). With none, Wave 0.
              Waves 0-4 are the classic Red ones; 5-14 are new. A rough guide
              (listen and tell us if one is wrong):
                 0 smooth, round        1 smooth, a little flatter
                 2 hollow, flute-like   3 reedy, wobbly
                 4 bright, buzzy        5 stepped, nasal
                 6 organ-like           7 gritty, jagged
                 8 rough, brassy        9 thin and quiet
                10 mellow, wide hump   11 bright sawtooth
                12 soft, low           13 pure, sine-like
                14 square (hollow)
  Example track names: "Ch1 Square 50", "Ch2 Square 25", "Ch3 Wave 2".
  DAWs that put text events in the MIDI (FL Studio, not MuseScore) can also
  use the effect markers in Template.mid, for example "duty_cycle 2". A
  marker in the MIDI wins over the name.

CHANGING THE SOUND MID-SONG
  Ch1, Ch2 and Ch3 can switch sound as often as you like (Ch1 from Square 50
  to Square 25 for a chorus, Ch3 from Wave 2 to Wave 10). A switch applies
  from the next note on. Two ways to write one:

  A. A PATCH TRACK (works in every program, including FL Studio)
     Add a track named "Ch1 Patch" (or Ch2 Patch, Ch3 Patch). In FL Studio,
     that's another channel in the Channel rack with that name; any
     instrument will do, it makes no sound in the game. Each note you put on
     it picks the sound for its channel, starting at that note's time. Only
     the note's pitch matters, not its length or velocity:

        Ch1 / Ch2 Patch            Ch3 Patch
        MIDI  FL name  sound       MIDI  FL name  sound    MIDI  FL name  sound
          0   C0   Square 12.5       4   E0   Wave 0        13   C#1  Wave 8
          1   C#0  Square 25         5   F0   Wave 1        14   D1   Wave 9
          2   D0   Square 50         6   F#0  Wave 2        15   D#1  Wave 10
          3   D#0  Square 75         7   G0   Wave 3        16   E1   Wave 11
                                     8   G#0  Wave 4        17   F1   Wave 12
                                    10   A#0  Wave 5        18   F#1  Wave 13
                                    11   B0   Wave 6        19   G1   Wave 14
                                    12   C1   Wave 7
     "FL name" is FL Studio's label, where middle C (MIDI 60) is C5. In a
     program that calls middle C "C4", every name is one octave lower (C-1
     instead of C0). The MIDI numbers are what count.
     The Patch track doesn't change what you hear in FL: to preview a switch,
     also change the preset on that channel's instrument at the same point,
     or listen in the tester.

  B. MIDI PROGRAM CHANGES, if your program writes them into the exported
     MIDI. Same numbers as the table above (they're also the SoundFont
     presets' numbers: 20-49, the DLS "Wave N 25%/50%" presets, pick Wave N;
     loudness still comes from velocity). Programs that aren't Game Boy
     sounds (Piano, Flute...) are ignored.

  Either way:
  - The track name sets the starting sound and wins over a switch on the
    first note; remove the sound from the name to let the switch choose.
  - A switch in the middle of a held note waits for the next note.
  - Looping is handled: the loop starts again on the sound it started with.
  - Ch4 has no sound switches (its drums pick their own sounds).
  The tester window lists every switch it made ("Ch3: the sound switches
  2 times (Wave 2, Wave 10)"). If your switches aren't listed, they didn't
  reach the exported MIDI.


THE LIMITS
----------
1. ONE NOTE AT A TIME PER TRACK. No chords on a single track. Spread chord
   notes over Ch1, Ch2 and Ch3 instead. The tester tells you the track, bar
   and beat of any chord it finds. In MuseScore, a staff with two voices
   playing together counts as a chord.

2. NOTE RANGE
     Ch1 and Ch2:  MIDI 36 to 127  (C2 and up)
     Ch3:          MIDI 24 to 119  (C1 to B8)
   Anything lower or higher is rejected with a message, because the converter
   would otherwise turn it into broken noise.

3. VOLUME
     Ch1 and Ch2: MIDI velocity selects volume 0-15, in groups of eight.
          1-7 = 0 (silent), 8-15 = 1, 16-23 = 2, 24-31 = 3,
          32-39 = 4, 40-47 = 5, 48-55 = 6, 56-63 = 7,
          64-71 = 8, 72-79 = 9, 80-87 = 10, 88-95 = 11,
          96-103 = 12, 104-111 = 13, 112-119 = 14, 120-127 = 15.
          Velocity 0 means note-off. Use MIDI numbers, not a DAW's percent
          display. In MuseScore, dynamics (p, mf, f) change the velocity.
     Ch3 (kit version 4): MIDI velocity 1-42 = 25% volume,
          43-84 = 50%, 85-127 = 100%. Velocity 0 is note-off.
          The hardware offers these three levels, not a smooth fade.
          Older testers forced full volume: install this complete new kit.
          Existing songs may sound quieter. To preserve their old balance,
          set Ch3 velocities to 85-127. The original MIDI is never changed.
          Native .asm songs keep their explicitly authored volume settings.
     Ch4: each drum has its own fixed envelope. Velocity does not make a
          drum quieter; it only chooses the winner of simultaneous hits.
     Mixer volume/CC automation does not export into the game's dynamics.

4. DRUMS (Ch4): ONE HIT AT A TIME, 19 SOUNDS.
   The game's drum kit has 19 noise sounds and no real kick drum. You can
   write drums two ways:
     a) Normal drums (General MIDI). Use MuseScore's drumset, or any drum
        kit in your program: kick, snare, hats, toms and cymbals are changed
        into the closest Game Boy sound. This is a first-guess mapping, so
        listen to the result and tell us what sounds wrong. If two drums land
        on the same beat, the louder one stays (the window says how many were
        dropped).
     b) The kit's own drums, MIDI notes 0 to 18 (very low notes; some
        programs call note 0 "C-1", others "C-2"). Kick on 36 and snare on 38
        are General MIDI, so they are changed as in (a); notes 19 to 34, and
        above 81, are rejected.

        MIDI  Sound          MIDI  Sound
         0    snare 1         10   snare 9
         1    snare 2         11   cymbal 1
         2    snare 3         12   cymbal 2
         3    snare 4         13   cymbal 3
         4    snare 5         14   muted snare 1
         5    triangle 1      15   triangle 3
         6    triangle 2      16   muted snare 2
         7    snare 6         17   muted snare 3
         8    snare 7         18   muted snare 4
         9    snare 8

5. LOOPING: THE "Loop" TRACK. Game music plays once from the top, then
   repeats a section forever. You choose where that section starts with an
   extra track named Loop:
     a) Template.mid already has one. In your own song, add any instrument
        and name it Loop (MuseScore 4: Instruments panel, "+", add any
        instrument, then rename it Loop).
     b) Put ONE note on the Loop track, on the first beat of the bar the
        music should jump back to. Any pitch, any length. The game never
        plays this track.
     c) Optional: a second note where the loop should END, if that is before
        the end of the song. Anything after it is never played.
     d) Drop the song. The black window says, for example:
          Loop: plays bars 1-74 once, then goes back to the start of bar 9
        Check that the bar number is the one you meant.
   No Loop track: the whole song repeats from bar 1.
   A note that is still sounding where the loop starts is split in two there
   (the window says so); start a fresh note on that bar if you hear a bump.
   (Typing "begin" as text in MuseScore does NOT work: MuseScore never puts
   text into its MIDI files. In FL Studio and other DAWs, text or marker
   events named begin and end work too, on any track.)

6. SIZE. After each run, the window prints "Song size: X of Y bytes". Y is the
   biggest song the game has room for, so stay under it (the tester prints
   the exact limit for this version). A song over the limit may still play in
   the tester, but it cannot go into the real game. Repeated phrases are
   stored once, so reusing identical sections saves space. Very long songs
   fail with "too long".

7. YOU HAVE ONE SONG SLOT. The tester replaces the title-screen music and
   all battle music each run. It does not keep or mix songs.


HEARING IT IN BATTLE
--------------------
Every battle in the test game plays your song, so you can hear it under
the menus, attacks and Pokemon cries, just like in the real game. The
quickest way in is FIGHT 2, a test battle:
  1. Drop your song. The game opens on its intro; wait for the title screen
     (or press Enter to skip ahead).
  2. On the title screen, HOLD Shift (Select) and press Enter (Start). A
     menu opens. (Without Shift held you get the normal game, which also
     plays your song in every battle, but takes longer to reach one.)
  3. Press the down arrow once to FIGHT 2, then S (the A button).
  4. It asks for a number (any number is fine; it picks the Pokemon). Press
     S again. A battle starts with your song.
  5. Play the battle with S (A) and A (B). When it ends, the next battle
     starts on its own. Close BGB when you are done.
  Things to listen for: notes dropping out when you press S, during attacks
  and during cries (see WHICH TRACK GETS THE MELODY), and how the loop
  sounds after a minute or two.

  BGB keys:  arrows = D-pad   S = A   A = B   Enter = Start   Shift = Select
  BGB is free (by beware, bgb.bircd.org), included unchanged in
  _engine\emulator. Right-click its window for options, including key setup
  (Options > Joypad). Victory music after a battle is the game's own.


GAME SONGS
----------
The "Game Songs" folder holds every song in the game (Red/Blue, Gold/Silver/
Crystal, Pokemon Stadium, Trading Card Game...), each as a .asm file with a
.txt instrument sheet of the same name. INDEX.txt lists them all.
  - The .txt lists, channel by channel, the sound each voice uses (with the
    tester's names, like "Square 75" or "Wave 4"), volumes and fades as
    MIDI velocities, vibrato, the notes' range, and the drums. Use it to copy
    a classic setup into your own MIDI.
  - Open the .asm in Crystal Tracker (in this kit) to see and hear every
    note. Open it from the Game Songs folder so the tracker finds the waves
    and drums.
  - Drop a game song's .asm onto DROP YOUR SONG HERE to hear it in the
    tester, for example to compare it with yours in battle. Songs marked
    [tracker only] in INDEX.txt use a command the tester doesn't take.


WRITING THE SONG FILE YOURSELF (optional, advanced)
---------------------------------------------------
If you work in a tracker or write music code by hand, you can skip MIDI and
drag a song file in the game's own format (a .asm file) onto the same
"DROP YOUR SONG HERE". Template.asm is a small working example: open it in a
text editor, change it, drag it on.

The tester checks the file and tells you in plain words what is wrong:
  - one song label ending in :: followed by "channel_count", with one
    "channel n, Label" line per voice;
  - every channel, sound_call, sound_jump and sound_loop must point at a label
    inside the same file (the song is stored in one piece);
  - only the engine's own music commands (no load_wave, INCLUDE or INCBIN).
Labels are renamed for you so two songs never clash. Drum kits are your job
here: toggle_noise 0 / toggle_noise 1 pick the kit (see Template.asm).
CRYSTAL TRACKER (included): a free song editor made for this format, in the
"Crystal Tracker" folder. Double-click "Crystal Tracker.exe", no install
needed. It is free software by dannye (see the files next to it), included
unchanged; the latest version is on its official GitHub release page.

  Using it with this kit: keep your .asm files in this folder (next to
  Template.asm) and open them from here. Crystal Tracker finds the game's
  waves and drums through the "Makefile" and "crysaudio" folder in this
  folder, so leave those two where they are. Template.asm is a good first file
  to open. Its playback is close to the game but not exact; drag the saved
  .asm onto "DROP YOUR SONG HERE" to hear the real thing. For Ch3, pick waves
  0-14 with note_type's third number; don't use load_wave (custom wave
  shapes), which the tester rejects.


HEARING IT WHILE YOU WRITE (optional)
-------------------------------------
Your program's piano will not sound like the Game Boy. The DAW Samples folder
fixes that. The tester is still the real test: these previews are approximate
(the SoundFont's pitch, volume and wave shapes are close but not identical to
the game), and what the game plays is authoritative.

  "Red Rogue Game Boy.sf2"   one SoundFont with everything. Presets:
        Square 12.5, Square 25, Square 50, Square 75   (for Ch1 and Ch2)
        Wave 0 ... Wave 14                             (for Ch3)
        Drums            the kit's 19 drums on notes 0-18, and on the
                         General MIDI drum notes, using the same mapping the
                         tester uses
        Game Boy Drums   the same drum kit as a General MIDI percussion kit
                         (bank 128), for drumset staves and drum channels.
     Notes outside the range a voice can play are silent on purpose.

  MuseScore:   copy the .sf2 into Documents\MuseScore4\SoundFonts (MuseScore 3:
               View > Synthesizer > Fluid > Add). Then open the Mixer and, for
               each instrument, choose the Red Rogue Game Boy sound.
               Export: File > Export > MIDI.
  FL Studio:   load Fruity LSD in a Mixer effect slot, then browse to
               "Red Rogue Game Boy.dls". Set its port to 1 and route MIDI Out
               channels to that same port. Use MIDI channels 1, 2, 3 and 10;
               select the two Squares, a Wave and Game Boy Drums in LSD.
               Name the MIDI tracks Ch1 Square 50, Ch2 Square 25, Ch3 Wave 6
               (or your chosen timbres), and Ch4. To switch sounds mid-song,
               add a "Ch1 Patch" (etc.) channel; see CHANGING THE SOUND
               MID-SONG. The tester window lists each switch it found.
               DLS square velocity response is approximate; use the exact
               MIDI table above and check the exported MIDI in the tester.
               DLS1 waves need manual preview selection: Wave N = 100%,
               Wave N 25% or Wave N 50% = quieter. Also set MIDI velocity;
               the patch alone does not change exported game volume.
               SF2/SFZ waves follow the three velocity bands automatically.
               Drum DLS presets ignore velocity for loudness.
               See DLS QUICK START.txt in DAW Samples for the full map.
  Other:       any program that can load a .sf2 SoundFont works the same way.
               Without SoundFont support, the .sfz files (free sfizz plugin)
               and looping .wav files in the three folders do the same job.

Steps differ a little between versions; the only thing that matters is getting
the SoundFont into the instrument.


IF SOMETHING GOES WRONG
-----------------------
Read the black window first; it says what to fix in plain words. Yellow lines
are notes about what the tester decided for you (which track became which
voice, how drums were changed), not errors. If a message does not make sense,
send us:
  - your .mid
  - _engine\output\last-error.txt
