RED ROGUE MUSIC TESTER
======================

Make a MIDI in your music program (MuseScore, FL Studio, anything that exports
.mid), drag it onto "DROP YOUR SONG HERE", and the game opens playing your song
on the title screen. No install needed.


QUICK START
-----------
1. Before extracting the zip: right-click it, Properties, tick "Unblock", OK.
   If Windows says it protected your PC, click "More info", then "Run anyway".
2. Open Template.mid in your program. It is a small working song with the four
   tracks already named. Write your song in them.
3. Export as a MIDI file (Type 1 / multi-track, not Type 0, if it asks).
4. Drag the .mid onto "DROP YOUR SONG HERE.bat". A black window shows what
   happens, then the game opens. The song starts after the intro. Press Start
   to skip the intro.
5. When a song is finished, send us the zip that appears in the folder
   "SEND THESE TO US" (open ATTRIBUTION.txt inside it first and fill in your
   name and the song title). It holds your original file, what the tester made
   from it, and the kit version, so we can put it in the game exactly as you
   heard it. Nothing from _engine. "KIT VERSION.txt" says which kit you have.
   "INSTRUMENTS AND DRUMS.txt" lists every sound at a glance.


THE FOUR TRACKS (it is a Game Boy, so there are only four voices)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Ch1  Square wave. Melody or lead.
Ch2  Square wave. Harmony or second lead.
Ch3  Soft wave. Usually bass.
Ch4  Noise. Drums only.

Every channel sounds at the pitch written in your program.

HOW THE TESTER FINDS YOUR TRACKS
  - Any track with Ch1, Ch2, Ch3 or Ch4 in its name is used for that voice.
    "Ch1 Square 50" and "Ch3 Wave 2 bass" both work. Other tracks are ignored
    (the window tells you which).
  - If no track is named like that, it uses your tracks in order: the first
    three instruments are Ch1, Ch2, Ch3, and the drums (the track on MIDI
    channel 10, where MuseScore puts its drumset) are Ch4. If your drums are
    on another channel, name the track Ch4.
  - More than three non-drum tracks and no names: it stops and asks you to
    merge some or name the ones you want.

PICKING THE SOUND: put it in the track name
  Ch1 / Ch2:  "Square 12.5", "Square 25", "Square 50" or "Square 75"
              (how thin the pulse is). Or "Duty 0" to "Duty 3" (same four,
              in that order). With neither, you get 12.5%.
  Ch3:        "Wave 0" to "Wave 14" (15 different shapes). With none, Wave 0.
              Waves 0-4 are the classic Red ones; 5-14 are new. A rough guide
              (listen and tell us if one is wrong):
                 0 smooth, round        1 smooth, a little flatter
                 2 hollow, flute-like   3 reedy, wobbly
                 4 bright, buzzy        5 stepped, nasal
                 6 organ-like           7 gritty, jagged
                 8 rough, brassy        9 thin and quiet
                10 mellow, wide hump   11 bright sawtooth
                12 soft, low           13 pure, sine-like
                14 square (hollow)
  Example track names: "Ch1 Square 50", "Ch2 Square 25", "Ch3 Wave 2".
  Programs that let you put text events in the MIDI can also use the effect
  markers in Template.mid, for example "duty_cycle 2". A marker in the MIDI
  wins over the name.


THE LIMITS
----------
1. ONE NOTE AT A TIME PER TRACK. No chords on a single track. Spread chord
   notes over Ch1, Ch2 and Ch3 instead. The tester tells you the track, bar
   and beat of any chord it finds. In MuseScore, a staff with two voices
   playing together counts as a chord.

2. NOTE RANGE
     Ch1 and Ch2:  MIDI 36 to 127  (C2 and up)
     Ch3:          MIDI 24 to 119  (C1 to B8)
   Anything lower or higher is rejected with a message, because the converter
   would otherwise turn it into broken noise.

3. VOLUME
     Ch1 and Ch2: velocity sets the volume (it has 8 steps, not 127). In
          MuseScore, dynamics (p, mf, f) change the velocity.
     Ch3: no volume control. It is always full volume, so velocity does
          nothing there. Put quiet parts on Ch1 or Ch2 instead.

4. DRUMS (Ch4): ONE HIT AT A TIME, 19 SOUNDS.
   The game's drum kit has 19 noise sounds and no real kick drum. You can
   write drums two ways:
     a) Normal drums (General MIDI). Use MuseScore's drumset, or any drum
        kit in your program: kick, snare, hats, toms and cymbals are changed
        into the closest Game Boy sound. This is a first-guess mapping, so
        listen to the result and tell us what sounds wrong. If two drums land
        on the same beat, the louder one stays (the window says how many were
        dropped).
     b) The kit's own drums, MIDI notes 0 to 18 (very low notes; some
        programs call note 0 "C-1", others "C-2"). Kick on 36 and snare on 38
        are General MIDI, so they are changed as in (a); notes 19 to 34, and
        above 81, are rejected.

        MIDI  Sound          MIDI  Sound
         0    snare 1         10   snare 9
         1    snare 2         11   cymbal 1
         2    snare 3         12   cymbal 2
         3    snare 4         13   cymbal 3
         4    snare 5         14   muted snare 1
         5    triangle 1      15   triangle 3
         6    triangle 2      16   muted snare 2
         7    snare 6         17   muted snare 3
         8    snare 7         18   muted snare 4
         9    snare 8

5. LOOPING. If your MIDI has no loop markers, the whole song repeats from the
   start (the window says so). To loop only part of it, put text markers named
   "begin" and "end" on every track; Template.mid shows how. Markers written
   as MIDI "marker" events (some programs call them time markers) work too.
   If your program cannot write text or marker events, the song just repeats
   whole, which is fine for testing.
   Never put a marker in the middle of a
   note. If you do, you get an error mentioning notes that "overlap other
   events".

6. SIZE. After each run, the window prints "Song size: X of Y bytes". Y is the
   biggest song the game has room for, so stay under it (the tester prints
   the exact limit for this version). A song over the limit may still play in
   the tester, but it cannot go into the real game. Repeated phrases are
   stored once, so reusing identical sections saves space. Very long songs
   fail with "too long".

7. YOU HAVE ONE SONG SLOT. The tester replaces the title-screen music each
   run. It does not keep or mix songs.


WRITING THE SONG FILE YOURSELF (optional, advanced)
---------------------------------------------------
If you work in a tracker or write music code by hand, you can skip MIDI and
drag a song file in the game's own format (a .asm file) onto the same
"DROP YOUR SONG HERE". Template.asm is a small working example: open it in a
text editor, change it, drag it on.

The tester checks the file and tells you in plain words what is wrong:
  - one song label ending in :: followed by "channel_count", with one
    "channel n, Label" line per voice;
  - every channel, sound_call, sound_jump and sound_loop must point at a label
    inside the same file (the song is stored in one piece);
  - only the engine's own music commands (no load_wave, INCLUDE or INCBIN).
Labels are renamed for you so two songs never clash. Drum kits are your job
here: toggle_noise 0 / toggle_noise 1 pick the kit (see Template.asm).
Crystal Tracker is a free tracker that exports this format. It is not
included in this kit; search for "Crystal Tracker" and download it from its
official release page.


HEARING IT WHILE YOU WRITE (optional)
-------------------------------------
Your program's piano will not sound like the Game Boy. The DAW Samples folder
fixes that. The tester is still the real test: these previews are approximate
(the SoundFont's pitch, volume and wave shapes are close but not identical to
the game), and what the game plays is authoritative.

  "Red Rogue Game Boy.sf2"   one SoundFont with everything. Presets:
        Square 12.5, Square 25, Square 50, Square 75   (for Ch1 and Ch2)
        Wave 0 ... Wave 14                             (for Ch3)
        Drums            the kit's 19 drums on notes 0-18, and on the
                         General MIDI drum notes, using the same mapping the
                         tester uses
        Game Boy Drums   the same drum kit as a General MIDI percussion kit
                         (bank 128), for drumset staves and drum channels.
     Notes outside the range a voice can play are silent on purpose.

  MuseScore:   copy the .sf2 into Documents\MuseScore4\SoundFonts (MuseScore 3:
               View > Synthesizer > Fluid > Add). Then open the Mixer and, for
               each instrument, choose the Red Rogue Game Boy sound.
               Export: File > Export > MIDI.
  FL Studio:   on a channel, load the Fruity SoundFont Player (or DirectWave)
               and open the .sf2; pick a preset per channel. Name the channels
               Ch1, Ch2, Ch3, Ch4 (add the sound, like "Ch1 Square 50", if you
               like), then File > Export > MIDI file.
  Other:       any program that can load a .sf2 SoundFont works the same way.
               Without SoundFont support, the .sfz files (free sfizz plugin)
               and looping .wav files in the three folders do the same job.

Steps differ a little between versions; the only thing that matters is getting
the SoundFont into the instrument.


IF SOMETHING GOES WRONG
-----------------------
Read the black window first; it says what to fix in plain words. Yellow lines
are notes about what the tester decided for you (which track became which
voice, how drums were changed), not errors. If a message does not make sense,
send us:
  - your .mid
  - _engine\output\last-error.txt
